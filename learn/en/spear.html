The Spear Variation: Two Similar Jumps, Opposite Results

kairo · November 18, 2026

The AI rates the Spear Variation as roughly equal. The interesting part is White's reply: of two jumps that look almost the same, one keeps the balance and the other clearly loses ground.

The moves so far

Black slides an edge stone diagonally, the Corner Opening (1f-2e), and White mirrors it with the Symmetrical Defense (1a-2b). Now Black pushes a second stone two squares straight ahead: the Spear Variation (2f-2d).

2d steps out in front of 2e, and column 2 gets a vertical line that looks like a spear. That is where the name comes from. The AI rates the position as roughly equal.

White has two good answers

When the AI reads every White reply, two of them keep the game equal.

The first is 6a-5b, sliding the opposite corner diagonally. White keeps its stones together and its numbers intact.

If you jump, jump to 4d

The other good move is the jump into the center, 2b-4d. The stone on 4d has no neighbors, so its number is 0. At first glance it is a loner.

But 4d sits right next to where Black wants to build. When Black develops naturally with 6f-5e and 5f-3d, Black stones gather around 4d, its number rises to 2, and 4d-6f gets White's first stone home. Black's own moves give White's loner a wake-up.

2a-4c is a real loner

The other jump into the center, 2a-4c, is a different story.

4c is far from Black's stones, so nothing ever lands next to it. It stays at 0 and cannot move, and White has simply wasted a move. The AI rates it Black +77.

Whether a jumped stone comes alive depends on how close it lands to where the opponent gathers stones

The main line continues

After 6a-5b, Black has two candidates.

  • 2d-3c (the spear tip steps diagonally): about equal. If White now jumps 2a-4c, that is the Pyrrhus Gambit.
  • 3f-3d (a center stone steps out): White is slightly better. If White now offers a goal with 2b-4d, that is the Cannae Gambit.

Summary

  • The Spear Variation (2f-2d) is roughly equal by the AI's evaluation.
  • White has two good answers: 6a-5b and 2b-4d.
  • The stone on 4d looks like a loner, but Black's natural development raises its number and gets White home first.
  • After 2a-4c the stone is a real loner, and Black is clearly better (+77).
  • The main line leads into the Pyrrhus Gambit and the Cannae Gambit.

Evaluations come from the AI using the same neural-network evaluation as the app's strongest CPU (8 seconds per position). The bigger the plus number, the better that side stands. The first version of this article used numbers from an older evaluation, so it was rewritten on November 20, 2026. In games between people, the AI's best move is not always the one that wins most often.
